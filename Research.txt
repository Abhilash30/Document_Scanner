Why normalize raw pixels?
- SVM is sensitive to the scale of input features - due to dot products
- Without normalization: Multiplying large pixel values (like \(255 \times 255\)) repeatedly across thousands of pixels can lead to extremely large numbers. This causes arithmetic overflow or numerical precision issues, causing the optimization algorithm to struggle to converge or fail completely.
To avoid overflow, computers use decimals (called "floating-point numbers"). However, these have their own trap: they lose precision when mixing massive numbers with tiny numbers.
In AI and image processing, algorithms calculate tiny adjustments (like 0.0001) to improve an image or model. If the computer has to add a tiny adjustment (0.0001) to a massive pixel product (like 16,000,000), it physically lacks the space to keep track of both. It rounds off the tiny number, treating it as if it were 0.
